Before we solve anything

What data is

Data comes in two kinds, and it almost always ends up in the same shape: rows and columns.

Numerical: you can measure or count it

  • Minutes waited in the lunch line
  • Songs played today
  • Points scored in a game

Categorical: labels or groups

  • Grade: 9th, 10th, 11th, 12th
  • How you get to school: bus, car, walk
  • Favorite club

Data lives in rows and columns

One row = one record (one person’s answers). One column = one thing asked of everyone. Every tool you will see today expects this shape.

Raw data → information

12, 8, 10  →  “The lunch-line wait averages 10 minutes.”

Raw data is the list. Information is what the list means.

Where to find real data

Governments, universities and companies publish huge datasets for free. Most open straight in Google Sheets, or as a map or chart in the browser.

Kaggle Datasets

Thousands of datasets on every topic, from sports to music to schools. Free account.

U.S. government open data: education, transportation, health, environment.

data.gov

Population, income and internet access for any town, county or state.

Which internet providers and speeds are available at any address. Data downloads too.

Your own school or city

School report cards, city open-data pages, club records. Ask who already counts it.

ask an adult who owns the data

Four questions before you trust a dataset

  1. Who collected it, and why?
  2. When was it collected? Is it still current?
  3. How much is there? Enough to say something?
  4. Can you use it? Check the license.

AI-assisted, no code

AI tools for data

You don’t need to program to get help from AI. Upload a spreadsheet to an AI assistant and ask questions in plain English.

Ask questions of your data

“Which location has the slowest average speed?” Upload the Sheet or CSV to Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini and ask.

Summarize open-ended answers

Paste 100 survey comments and ask: “What are the top three complaints?”

Find messy data

“Find typos, duplicates and impossible values in this sheet.”

Suggest a chart

“What chart best shows speed by time of day?” Then build it in Sheets or Canva.

Three rules for using AI with data

  1. Check one number yourself. AI can be confidently wrong. Compare it with your pivot table.
  2. Keep private data out. No names, emails or student IDs in the upload.
  3. Say when you used AI in your problem brief.

Why data still matters with AI

AI assistants were built by learning patterns from enormous amounts of data. That is why the same rule applies to them as to you: bad data in, bad answers out.

In Mission 4 you add one AI feature to your app. The data you collect now is what that feature will work with.
